# Story sheet – "Who Survives If the World Economy Collapses?"

Emotion word: dread → then the strange relief of understanding (awe at the pattern).
Dramatic question: If the AI bubble pops and drags the whole world down, which country comes out on top, and what happens to your money on the way?
Reframed question (~2/3): Everyone runs to the country that caused it. Will they still run there next time, or have the money printers already started buying gold?
Protagonist: YOU, holding $10,000 in a retirement index fund (tracked on screen), plus micro-people around the world (salesman Ohio, trader Tokyo, baker Cairo, captain Shanghai, teller Berlin 1931 dramatized).
Misconception voiced: "Gold will save me." / "When it crashes, cash is worthless."
Wrong belief #2: "The country that starts it loses."

ABT: The world is already shaky (Hormuz war, oil ~$90, Fed hiking, ~$500B of new AI debt this year) AND AI firms lend to, invest in and buy from each other, BUT when one link breaks every safe place fails in order (stocks, gold, banks, trade), THEREFORE the winner is decided by things you can't print, and the money runs into the burning house anyway, unless the central banks buying gold are right.

Spine: Once upon a time the world ran on cheap AI debt. Every day, money went around in a circle. One day one company missed one payment. Because of that the chips lost value and big tech fell. Because of that your fund fell and margin calls sold gold. Because of that the rich stopped spending, the yen loan unwound, the dollar soared and bread in Cairo got expensive. Because of that a bank failed and countries walled themselves off. Until finally the central banks printed, and the world picked a winner by food, energy, factories, army and trust. Ever since, the question is whether it will pick America again.

Ending (final image): The 1944 hotel → "In 1944 the invitations went out from Washington. The central banks buying gold right now are betting the next ones won't." Your $10,000 counter shown one last time.
Planted detail: the $10,000 counter (one plant, paid at the end).
Honest-limit clause: "That company is made up. Everything after it has already happened somewhere."
