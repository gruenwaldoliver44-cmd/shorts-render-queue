# MT3 · Smoke detector is radioactive (template: refs/Tqj_iQORgLI + gas pump causal chain)
There's a tiny piece of radioactive metal on your ceiling, and it's there to save your life. You see, inside most smoke detectors sits a small button of americium, a metal made in nuclear reactors. It constantly fires tiny particles into an air chamber. They knock electrons off the air, so a tiny current flows between two metal plates. But when smoke drifts in, it soaks up that current. The current drops, and the alarm goes off. And half of that little button will still be there in four hundred years.
Facts: NRC smoke detector fact sheet (ionization chamber, Am-241 <=1 microcurie, plates, current, smoke reduces current); CDC Am-241 brief (half-life 432.2 y, present in smoke detectors); americium made in reactors (Wikipedia). Proof: Branch Education "Why are Smoke Detectors Radioactive?" 1.19M.
