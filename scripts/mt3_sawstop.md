## Script (~113 words, ~38-41 s)
1. This inventor pushed his own finger into a spinning table saw, on purpose.
2. His blade carries a tiny electric signal.
3. Wood barely changes it. Skin does, because your body conducts electricity.
4. When the signal drops, a current melts a wire holding back a spring.
5. The spring slams an aluminum block into the blade. It stops in under five milliseconds.
6. The blade's own spin then yanks it down under the table.
7. Instead of losing a finger, you usually get a small cut.
8. But none of the big saw makers would take the deal.
9. So in 2004, he started building the saws himself.
10. The price of saving a finger? A new blade and a new brake cartridge.
